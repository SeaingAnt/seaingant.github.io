  <h2>Background & Vision</h2>
  <p>
    Building on my doctoral research in hybrid learning-control approaches, my work aims to shift multi-robot systems from passive observation to active physical intervention. Because deploying "black box" models on physical systems introduces risk, I advocate for a <strong>hybrid approach</strong>: conditioning learned policies with provable control structures. By embedding first-order principles inherently into the design, we can achieve rigorous safety, stability, and sim-to-real transferability.
  </p>

  <h2>Core Research Focus</h2>
  <p>
    My current work bridges theoretical guarantees and real-world application through the following thrusts:
  </p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Hybrid Learning & Control:</strong> Reconciling classical control techniques (e.g., Lyapunov, Contraction Theory) with deep learning to guarantee safety in neural policies.</li>
    <li><strong>Functional Heterogeneity:</strong> Formulating metrics and dynamic coalition frameworks to understand how diverse agents can optimize complex tasks better than homogeneous swarms.</li>
    <li><strong>Graph Neural Networks (GNNs):</strong> Analyzing GNNs as distributed dynamical systems to ensure Input-to-State Stability (ISS) in multi-agent communication.</li>
    <li><strong>Environment Co-Design & Manipulation:</strong> Enabling robot teams to actively reshape their surroundings and collaboratively manipulate complex, deformable objects in unstructured environments.</li>
  </ul>
